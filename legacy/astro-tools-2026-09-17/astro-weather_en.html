Astro Weather

Seven days of forecast at a glance, made for one question: is the night worth it? Clouds at three heights, estimated seeing and transparency, wind, temperature and dew point – plus sun and moon altitude with the moon phase. In Europe the weather data come from the ICON model of Germany’s national weather service, the DWD, elsewhere from the US models GFS and HRRR; a second cloud row shows the ECMWF model for comparison.

How to read the chart

Every hour gets an overall rating from 0 to 100 %: from 85 % Excellent, from 65 % Good, from 45 % Average, from 25 % Poor, below that Very poor. Clouds decide almost all of it: half covered means 25 % at best.

The seven-day chart

  • Moon: symbol with the illuminated share, above its highest point.
  • Sun and moon: yellow times the sun, white the moon, below them their altitude. Light columns day and twilight, dark ones astronomical night, red line Now.
  • Hour bar: one symbol for the six hours around it, the most severe weather wins. “Mainly clear” allows almost 50 % cloud.
  • Overall, clouds, ECMWF, seeing, transp.: the stronger the blue, the better, dark grey means no data. ECMWF is the second model: agreement means a reliable forecast.
  • Wind in km/h with a direction arrow: green to 10, yellow to 30, red from 40.
  • Temperature (orange) and dew point (blue): close together means dew on the optics.
  • Day bar: the rating hour by hour, above it the night’s average.

Table and night detail

One row per night, noon to noon: astronomically dark, of that dark with the moon down, the rating of those hours, the lit share of the moon, its rise (↑) and set (↓). Clicking opens the night: weather per hour, the twilight band with a light-blue strip for the moonless time, then the values per hour – overall, seeing, clouds by height (low to 2 km, mid to 6 km, high above), ECMWF, AOD, water vapour, dust, visibility, rain – and below them wind, gusts, temperature, dew point with their spread and the humidity. The spread turns orange below 4 °C, red below 2 °C. Above the chart stands the weather model, the arrows switch nights, a tap shows every value of an hour.

Terms

  • Astronomically dark: the sun more than 18° below the horizon – it gets no darker here.
  • Seeing: how steady the air is. Unsteady air makes stars twinkle and blurs planetary detail.
  • Transparency: how clear the air is. Haze swallows the light of galaxies and nebulae.
  • AOD: haze and dust through the whole air column. Around 0.1 very clear, from 0.3 milky.
  • Water vapour (mm): below 10 dry, above 30 humid.
  • Dust (µg/m³): mineral dust near the ground, turns the sky milky.
  • Dew point: once the air reaches it, lenses and mirrors fog up.

Where the values come from – and where they are estimates

Clouds, wind, temperature, humidity and the weather symbols come straight from a weather model, up to seven days ahead. Which one depends on the location and stands above the night detail.

  • Europe (29.5 to 70.5° N, 23.5° W to 62.5° E): the DWD’s ICON – first ICON-D2 at 2.2 km grid spacing, then ICON-EU at 7 km, last ICON global at 11 km, which carries no visibility.
  • Rest of the world: NOAA – about two days of HRRR at 3 km in North America, GFS at 13 km elsewhere. The reason: over 30 nights at Starfront, GFS and HRRR sat closer to the airports’ hourly cloud reports than ICON.
  • The ECMWF row: cloud cover from the IFS model at 9 km, worldwide, as a second opinion. It does not feed into the overall rating.

Three rows are estimates. Seeing comes from no model; the chart derives it from the wind profile: the jet stream at about 10 km, the shear between 1.5 and 10 km, and the wind at the ground. Transparency comes from the aerosol optical depth (AOD) of the Copernicus CAMS forecast, damped by high humidity and much water vapour; CAMS reaches four to five days, after which the row stays dark grey. Overall is a weighting of my own in which clouds decide almost everything. All three catch the tendency, no more.

Sun and moon are calculated by your browser; planets and the best objects of a night are shown by the Observing Planner. The weather map is an embedded meteoblue service with their own data, not part of these calculations.
